## The model

1. solar.ts: where the sun is (short)
solarZenithDeg: the sun's angle from straight overhead (0° is overhead, 90° is the horizon), computed by suncalc. Every later step depends on this angle.
sunEarthDistanceFactor: Earth is about 3% closer to the sun in January, so sunlight is about 3% stronger then.
2. clearSky.ts: UV on a cloudless day (step 1)
allaartClearSkyUVI is the Allaart formula: sun angle plus ozone gives UVI.
mu0, mux and uva cover how much UV gets through the atmosphere.
X and the F·X^G + H/TO + J bracket cover how much ozone absorbs.
There are two parameter sets: the original 2004 paper and the 2017 TEMIS refit, which is the default.
Altitude adds 5% per km.
clearSkyUVIAt is the convenience version that takes a date and location.
3. cmf.ts and cmfParams.ts: what clouds remove (step 2)
cloudModificationFactor takes low, mid and high cloud cover (each 0–1) and returns a multiplier from 0 to 1. A value of 1 means clouds aren't blocking anything.
The aᵢ and bᵢ numbers live in cmfParams.ts. That file is generated by fit.py, so don't edit it by hand.
4. uvSource.ts: where UVI(t) comes from
fetchForecast calls the Open-Meteo API and returns hourly UV and cloud layers, from 2 days back (for logging time after the fact) to 7 days ahead (for the planner).
readUV answers "what's the UV right now?" in one of three modes:
'forecast': use Open-Meteo's UVI directly. It already includes clouds, so the CMF isn't applied again.
'physics': our Allaart clear-sky × our CMF, using only Open-Meteo's cloud layers.
'demo': fully offline, with clouds you choose. This is what you'd show judges.
It also sanity-checks the API: if the forecast is impossibly high, it clamps the value and sets clamped: true.
5. exposure.ts: what actually hits your skin (step 3)
UVI is measured on a flat, horizontal surface, but skin points in all directions.
geometryFactor (G) converts horizontal UV to UV on skin:
Posture: upright (face and arms, G ≈ 0.35 at midday), shoulders, or lying (G = 1).
Direct vs scattered light: most UV arrives scattered from the whole sky rather than straight from the sun.
Ground reflection (SURFACES): negligible for grass, large for snow.
Shade (SHADES): none, umbrella or dense. Shade blocks direct sun and part of the sky, but not reflection from the ground, so an umbrella on sand only halves your dose. The shade values are assumptions.
skinIrradiance gives E = 0.025 · UVI · G, in W/m².
6. sunscreen.ts: what sunscreen blocks (step 4)
Effective SPF is the labelled SPF raised to three shrinking exponents:
applicationExponent: you applied less than the 2 mg/cm² used in lab testing.
photoRetention: UV breaks down the film. The chemical type degrades and photostable barely does.
wearRetention: rubbing, sweat and water. It halves about every 1.4 h when dry, and faster when swimming.
A FilmState tracks one application: how much UV has hit it and how worn it is.
effectiveSPF combines those into the current SPF.
7. dose.ts: adding it all up (step 5)
This is the main engine.

MED_SED: the burn dose for each skin type, from 2.5 SED (type I) to 10 SED (type VI).
SessionState holds the dose so far plus the sunscreen film.
advance(state, seconds, env, settings) steps time forward. Each second outside adds UV ÷ SPF to your dose and wears the sunscreen.
burnFraction is dose ÷ (your MED × c_user). At 1.0 you burn.
project simulates forward using the forecast and answers "how many minutes until I burn?".
ALERT_THRESHOLDS sets notifications at 50%, 80% and 100%.
PRESETS bundles posture, surface and activity for one-tap outings (walk, run, picnic, beach, pool, ski).
WORST_SITE_POSTURES lists the sites tracked side by side (face/arms and shoulders). Shoulders usually burn first, in about a third of the time at midday.
explainRate breaks the current dose rate into its factors (UVI, G, effective SPF) for the "Why this number?" screen.
8. calibration.ts: learning your real skin
updateCUser: the next morning the app asks "any redness?" and adjusts c_user.
You burned earlier than predicted: lower c_user, decisively.
You reached the predicted limit with no redness: raise it a little.
calibrationPromptTime schedules that question for 9am the next day, because redness peaks 8–24 h after exposure.
9. index.ts
Re-exports everything, so the app can write import { project, readUV } from '@/model'.

The fit pipeline (offline; not part of the app)
10. model-fit/fetch.ts
Downloads 2025 hourly UV and cloud data for 12 cities and 4 seasons from Open-Meteo, then runs our clearSky.ts on every hour. The output is data/hourly.csv.

11. model-fit/fit.py
Fits the aᵢ and bᵢ cloud parameters with scipy, constrained so full overcast gives a 50–60% reduction. It writes three files:

cmfParams.ts, used by the app
report.md, numbers for the slide
cmf_fit.png, the figure
12. model-fit/README.md and report.md
How to re-run the fit, the decisions the data forced, and the "say this on the slide" limitations.

Tests
13. model.test.ts
One test per physical claim, for example:

overhead sun gives about 11.8
1 h at UVI 8 lying unprotected gives 7.2 SED
calibration moves c_user in the right direction
Run them with npm run test:model from app/. They also work as runnable examples of how to call each function.

What isn't built yet: the UI. No screen calls any of this; the files in src/app/ are still the Expo template. The next step is a home screen that gets your location, calls fetchForecast and readUV, and runs advance on a timer while you're outside.
